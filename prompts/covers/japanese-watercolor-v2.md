# Japanese watercolor engineering cover, version 2

Use case: stylized-concept
Asset type: portrait magazine cover artwork
Primary request: Create a minimal Japanese-inspired watercolor painting that reflects an issue about jet engines, titanium, robotic dexterity, and subsea inspection.
Scene/backdrop: Natural warm ivory washi paper with visible fine fibers and abundant untouched negative space.
Subject: One sparse, abstract mechanical study. A pale titanium turbine ring sits partly submerged in an indigo wash. Three fine articulated lines suggest a robotic hand reaching toward it. A single thin vermilion inspection cable descends into the water. Merge the subjects into one quiet composition rather than four separate illustrations.
Style/medium: Traditional sumi-e sensibility with translucent Japanese watercolor. Use soft pigment blooms, feathered wet edges, slight spillover, restrained brush pressure, and a few imperfect pools of diluted color.
Composition/framing: Portrait magazine cover with calm asymmetrical balance. Concentrate the artwork in the lower two-thirds. Leave the upper area quiet for the issue mark.
Color palette: Carbon black, smoky gray, muted titanium blue, a deep desaturated indigo wash, and one restrained vermilion line. Natural ivory remains dominant.
Text (verbatim): "ISSUE 1"
Text treatment: The only text in the image. Use small, crisp, understated uppercase lettering near the top center with generous spacing.
Constraints: Keep the engineering references legible but abstract. The watercolor must look absorbed into real paper. No border, logo, magazine title, date, author name, article title, decorative writing, or extra text.
Avoid: Photorealism, exploded diagrams, technical labels, digital gradients, glossy metal, busy machinery, ornate Japanese motifs, people, buildings, calligraphy, seals, watermarks, mockup shadows, and any text other than "ISSUE 1".
