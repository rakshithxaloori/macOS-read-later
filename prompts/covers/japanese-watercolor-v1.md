# Japanese watercolor cover, version 1

Use case: stylized-concept
Asset type: portrait magazine cover artwork
Primary request: Create a very minimal Japanese-inspired watercolor painting for a private literary magazine issue.
Scene/backdrop: Natural warm ivory washi paper with visible fine fibers and abundant untouched negative space.
Subject: A single quiet ink-and-watercolor gesture suggesting a distant mountain, one slender pine, and a low morning sun. Keep the forms sparse and abstract rather than illustrative.
Style/medium: Traditional sumi-e sensibility combined with translucent Japanese watercolor. Soft pigment blooms, feathered wet edges, slight spillover, restrained brush pressure, and a few imperfect pools of diluted color.
Composition/framing: Portrait magazine cover, calm asymmetrical balance, large areas of empty paper, artwork concentrated in the lower two-thirds. Leave the upper area quiet for the issue mark.
Color palette: Carbon black and smoky gray ink, one muted vermilion sun, faint desaturated indigo wash. Natural ivory remains dominant.
Text (verbatim): "ISSUE 1"
Text treatment: The only text in the image. Small, crisp, understated uppercase lettering near the top center, with generous spacing.
Constraints: Minimal and contemplative. The watercolor must look absorbed into real paper. Preserve a refined editorial feel. No border, no logo, no magazine title, no dates, no article names, no extra writing.
Avoid: Photorealism, digital gradients, glossy texture, busy scenery, ornate Japanese motifs, people, buildings, calligraphy, seals, watermarks, mockup shadows, and any text other than "ISSUE 1".
